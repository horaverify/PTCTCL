\section{Background}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Symbolic states, zones, and DBMs}

A network of timed automata over clocks $\clocks = \{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}$ has
\emph{symbolic states} $(q, Z)$, where $q$ is the discrete part (the location
vector plus integer variables) and $Z$ is a \emph{zone}: a convex set of clock
valuations defined by constraints $x_i - x_j \prec b_{ij}$ with
$\prec \in \{<,\leq\}$.  Zones are stored as Difference Bound
Matrices~\cite{Dill89}: an $(n{+}1)\times(n{+}1)$ matrix $Z$ over
$(\mathbb{Z} \times \{<,\leq\}) \cup \{\infty\}$ with $x_0 \equiv 0$ the
reference clock.  We write $Z[i][j]$ for the entry bounding $x_i - x_j$ and
order entries by the usual $\preceq$ on bounds.

A DBM is \emph{closed} (canonical) when every entry is the tightest derivable
one, i.e.\ $Z[i][j] \preceq Z[i][k] + Z[k][j]$ for all $i,j,k$.  Closure is
computed by Floyd--Warshall in $O(n^3)$ and is a precondition for essentially
every zone operation; in particular, for closed $Z, Z'$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:denseincl}
  Z \subseteq Z' \iff \forall i,j:\; Z[i][j] \preceq Z'[i][j],
\end{equation}
an $O(n^2)$ test.  \emph{Closure is the expensive operation, and its cost is the
hinge of this paper.}

The successor function $\succfn(q,Z)$ applies, for each enabled edge: guard
intersection, clock resets, target invariant, time elapse, and LU$^+$
extrapolation~\cite{Behrmann06}, discarding empty results.  It reads only the
(globally read-only) network and $(q,Z)$; it never touches the passed list.

\subsection{Minimal constraint systems}\label{sec:mingraph}

Most entries of a closed DBM are \emph{redundant}: implied by shortest paths
through the others.  UPPAAL's UDBM therefore never stores the dense matrix in
the passed list.  It stores the \emph{minimal constraint system} (equivalently,
the minimal graph)~\cite{LarsenLPY97}: the smallest set $G$ of constraints whose
closure is $Z$.  For a diagonal-free zone $|G|$ is $O(n)$ in practice, against
$n^2$ dense entries; at $n = 17$ this is roughly a $12\times$ reduction in
resident passed-set memory.  \tool{} uses the same representation, ported into
its own raw-bound encoding.

The representation is lossless: $\mathrm{close}(G) = Z$ exactly.  But it is
\emph{not} directly operable.  To run the zone algebra on a stored zone one must
first \emph{unpack} it---scatter the constraints of $G$ into an all-$\infty$
matrix and close it---so recovering a stored zone costs a full $O(n^3)$ closure.
\Cref{sec:filter} shows how to avoid paying it on the hot path.

\subsection{Subsumption and the \texorpdfstring{$aLU$}{aLU} abstraction}

Exploration prunes a candidate $(q,Z)$ when the passed list already holds
$(q,Z')$ that \emph{subsumes} it.  The cheapest sound choice is plain inclusion
$Z \subseteq Z'$.  Stronger pruning comes from the $\preceq_{aLU}$ preorder of
Herbreteau et al.~\cite{Herbreteau12}, which compares $Z$ against the
$LU$-abstraction of $Z'$ using the per-clock maximal lower/upper guard constants
$L, U$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alu}
  Z \preceq_{aLU} Z' \iff \neg\exists\, x,y:\;
     Z[0][x] \succeq (\leq, -U_x) \;\wedge\;
     Z'[y][x] \prec Z[y][x] \;\wedge\;
     Z'[y][x] + (<, -L_y) \prec Z[0][x].
\end{equation}
$\preceq_{aLU}$ is coarser than inclusion---it prunes strictly more---and is what
\tool{} uses whenever the model is diagonal-free.  Two properties matter later.
It reads \emph{arbitrary} entries $Z'[y][x]$ of the stored zone, so it needs $Z'$
dense and closed.  And, being coarser, it is implied by inclusion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incl-implies-alu}
  Z \subseteq Z' \;\Longrightarrow\; Z \preceq_{aLU} Z' .
\end{equation}
\Cref{eq:incl-implies-alu} is immediate from \cref{eq:denseincl}: inclusion gives
$Z[y][x] \preceq Z'[y][x]$ for all $y,x$, so the witness conjunct $Z'[y][x] \prec
Z[y][x]$ in \cref{eq:alu} is unsatisfiable.  It is the hinge of
\cref{sec:filter}.

\subsection{The sequential exploration loop}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Sequential zone-graph BFS (UPPAAL-style)}\label{alg:seq}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require network $\mathcal{N}$, target predicate $\varphi$
\State $Z_0 \leftarrow \mathit{init}(\mathcal{N})$;\enspace
       $\Passed[q_0] \leftarrow \{Z_0\}$;\enspace
       $\Wait \leftarrow \langle (q_0, Z_0) \rangle$
\While{$\Wait \neq \emptyset$}
  \State $(q, Z) \leftarrow \Wait.\mathbf{pop}()$
  \ForAll{$(q', Z') \in \succfn(q, Z)$}
    \If{$\varphi(q', Z')$} \Return \textbf{true} \EndIf
    \If{$\exists Z_p \in \Passed[q'] : Z' \preceq_{aLU} Z_p$}
      \textbf{continue}
    \EndIf
    \State $\Passed[q'] \mathrel{+}= Z'$;\quad
           $\Wait.\mathbf{push}(q', Z')$
                                              \Comment{\textit{shared writes}}
  \EndFor
\EndWhile
\Return \textbf{false}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\Cref{alg:seq} is the procedure both UPPAAL and \tool{}'s sequential path
implement (see \cref{fig:seq-bfs}).  Line~4 reads exactly the structure line~6
writes, so consecutive iterations carry a read-after-write dependency and cannot
overlap.  That dependency---not the cost of $\succfn$---is the source of
serialisation.

Two refinements in \tool{} matter here.  First, $\Passed[q]$ is maintained as an
\emph{antichain}: a stored zone subsumed by an incoming one is retired, and where
the union of two zones is exactly convex they are merged into their hull.
Antichains therefore stay short; \cref{sec:diagnosis} reports how short.  Second,
distinct zones are \emph{interned} in a shared table, so the many discrete states
reaching the same zone share one packed record, and the table is reclaimed by
mark-and-sweep when records fall out of use.
