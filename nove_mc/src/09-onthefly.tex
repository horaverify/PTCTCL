\section{Algorithm 2: making the greatest fixpoint on-the-fly}
\label{sec:otf}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} has one structural defect, and it is not a constant factor. Phase~1 runs to
completion before Phase~3 begins, so the cost of deciding \texttt{p --> q} is a function of the
\emph{model} and never of the \emph{query}. A leads-to property violated three transitions from the
initial state still pays for every reachable symbolic state. This section removes that defect
without weakening anything proved in \S\ref{sec:soundness}.

\subsection{Why this looks harder than it is}

$EG$ is a \emph{greatest} fixpoint, so the standard iteration starts at $\top$ and descends. An
intermediate iterate satisfies $W \supseteq F(W)$ --- it is a \textbf{pre}-fixpoint, an
over-approximation --- and therefore says nothing: ``$W_v \neq \emptyset$'' cannot settle a
violation, so there is no early exit. Worse, the moment the graph underneath grows, the values stop
descending, and the natural reading is that an incremental version needs a new convergence
argument.

It does not, because a violation never needed the fixpoint. It needs a \emph{certificate}, and by
Knaster--Tarski the certificates of a greatest fixpoint are exactly its \textbf{post}-fixpoints.
This is the same instrument already used once, in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound-holds}; here
it is used in the other role.

\begin{theorem}[A pass over a fragment is a certificate]
\label{thm:fragment}
Let $G$ be any subgraph explored so far and let $S$ be any set of \textbf{expanded} $\lnot q$
vertices of $G$. Define $F_S$ exactly as $F$ but with $W_u$ pinned to $\emptyset$ for every
$u \notin S$, and let $\nu F_S$ be its greatest fixpoint. Then
\[
   \nu F_S \;\subseteq\; \nu F \;=\; W^\infty ,
\]
where $F$ is the operator on the \emph{finished} graph. Consequently $\nu F_S(v) \neq \emptyset$
at some $v \models p \wedge \lnot q$ settles the query as \textsc{violated}, with exactly the
verdict Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} would have produced. \proved{}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$F_S$ is obtained from $F$ by deleting disjuncts: every $\Pre_e(W_{v'})$ with $v' \notin S$ is
replaced by $\Pre_e(\emptyset) = \emptyset$, and no term is added. Hence $F_S(X) \subseteq F(X)$
pointwise for every $X$. Both operators are monotone, so $\nu F_S \subseteq \nu F$ by
Knaster--Tarski: $\nu F_S$ is a post-fixpoint of $F_S$, hence of $F$, hence below the greatest
post-fixpoint of $F$.
\end{proof}

Two consequences, and they are the whole design.

\begin{itemize}
  \item A confined pass over a \emph{fragment} of the graph produces a sound under-approximation of
        the answer on the graph that has not been built yet. Non-empty settles \textsc{violated}
        immediately; the rest of the state space is never constructed.
  \item Taking $S$ to be every expanded $\lnot q$ vertex of the \emph{finished} graph gives
        $F_S = F$, so the final pass \emph{is} Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp}. Nothing is given up when
        the property holds.
\end{itemize}

No value has to descend across passes: each pass is a self-contained certificate search, restarted
from $\top$ on its own carrier, and monotonicity in the graph is free.

\begin{remark}[The one restriction, and it is the only one]
\label{rem:expanded}
A vertex may enter $S$ only once it has been \textbf{expanded}. The stopping term $\mathrm{stop}(v)$
reads the \emph{absence} of successors ($\mathrm{adj}(v) = \emptyset$), and an unexpanded vertex has
an empty adjacency for the wrong reason; admitting one would add a disjunct rather than delete one
and break the inclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:fragment}. Every other term survives a partial graph
untouched, because the $\Pre$ disjuncts only ever need edges that \emph{exist}, never the absence of
one. That asymmetry --- absence of edges is dangerous, presence is not --- is what makes backward
work on a half-built graph legitimate at all.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A capped pass proves nothing]
\label{rem:capped}
Theorem~\ref{thm:fragment} is about the \emph{limit} $\nu F_S$. An iterate stopped early --- by the
federation-width cap of \S\ref{sec:eval:cap} or an iteration bound --- is a pre-fixpoint of $F_S$
and is therefore neither an over- nor an under-approximation of anything. Reading such a value as
``no certificate found'' silently converts an exhausted budget into a \textsc{holds} verdict. The
implementation reports \textsc{unusable} instead, and the differential harness scores it as the
absence of a verdict rather than as agreement. \measured{}
\end{remark}

\subsection{The design space}
\label{sec:otf:space}

Five schemes were implemented behind one switch and measured against Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} on the
same graphs. They are reported together, including the three that lost, because which of them loses
is not predictable from the outside.

\paragraph{D1, pipelined-conservative.} Begin backward work only on vertices provably \emph{frozen}
--- expanded, and unable to reach an unexpanded vertex, computed by a reverse sweep from the
unexpanded set. For those, $F_S = F$ already holds, so their values are final rather than merely
certificates.

\paragraph{D2, append-only.} Refuse the in-place enlargement that Phase~1 performs when a newcomer
covers a stored zone ($\Zn_j \gets \Zn_{\text{new}}$), storing the newcomer beside the old vertex so
every stored vertex is immutable from birth.

\paragraph{D3, fully incremental.} Run a confined pass over the whole accepting closure after every
BFS level, relying on Theorem~\ref{thm:fragment} for each.

\paragraph{D4, preprocessing overlap.} Compute the per-vertex liveness data ($p$, $\lnot q$, the
stopping term) during expansion rather than in a post-pass, leaving the $EG$ iteration itself for
the frozen graph.

\paragraph{D5, candidate-confirm.} A forward DFS on the fully subsumed graph acts as a
\emph{candidate generator} --- it follows \textbf{every} edge, including subsumption edges --- and
each candidate ($\lnot q$ cycle, or stopping vertex) is confirmed by an exact confined pass over the
DFS stack. A confirmed candidate is a certificate; a refuted one costs one small pass.

\begin{remark}[D1 requires D2, and that is the difficulty of D1]
\label{rem:d1-needs-d2}
With in-place enlargement, ``this vertex will never change'' is not establishable before the forward
phase terminates: an enlargement invalidates the adjacency the backward operator has just read.
Making it establishable means refusing enlargement --- that is, adopting D2 --- and refusing
enlargement is precisely what costs states (\S\ref{sec:otf:eval}). The conjecture that a
conservative freeze criterion would be hard to establish is confirmed, and the obstruction is the
antichain-collapsing enlargement, not the shape of the graph. \measured{}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Why D5 can follow subsumption edges when the forward engine cannot}
\label{sec:otf:d5}

The shipping forward engine is already on-the-fly: a lazy Tarjan with eager escape over the $\lnot
q$ subgraph. But it must \emph{refuse} subsumption edges whenever it argues that a run continues
forever, because a redirected edge closes cycles the concrete system cannot execute --- the failure
of \S\ref{sec:liveness}. That refusal is not free: the search walks further before it can conclude.

D5 has no such constraint. It uses the forward search only to \emph{propose}, and every proposal is
adjudicated by the exact backward operator, whose soundness under redirection is
\S\ref{sec:soundness:asym}. So D5 keeps the fully subsumed graph forward \emph{and} exactness
backward. Neither half has both alone, and the measured gap appears exactly where predicted: on the
violated queries whose witness is a \emph{cycle} rather than a stopping vertex
(\S\ref{sec:otf:eval}).

\subsection{Algorithm 3: the hull lane}
\label{sec:otf:d6}

D1--D5 all decide both directions of the verdict with one graph, so all of them need that graph to
be precise enough for the \emph{harder} direction. Theorem~\ref{thm:sound-holds} says this is
unnecessary, and says it in a stronger form than \S\ref{sec:soundness} used:

\begin{quote}
$\Omega \subseteq W^\infty$ is \textbf{unconditional}. It does not use (A2), and it does not care
how badly $\Zn_v$ over-approximates $\Reach(v)$, because an over-approximation only enlarges
$W^\infty$, which is the safe direction for concluding \textsc{holds}.
\end{quote}

If the forward abstraction may be arbitrarily coarse and still prove \textsc{holds}, then take it to
the limit. \textbf{Algorithm 3} stores exactly \textbf{one vertex per reachable discrete state},
whose zone is the convex hull of every zone that ever arrives there:
\[
   \Zn_v \;\gets\; \mathrm{hull}(\Zn_v, \Zn_{\text{new}})
   \qquad\text{(entrywise max of canonical DBMs, itself canonical).}
\]
This is the coarsest zone abstraction there is, and it puts the forward graph on the
\textbf{discrete floor}: one symbolic state per reachable discrete state is the least any zone-based
method can store.

\begin{proposition}[Algorithm 3 is one-sided, and sound in the direction it claims]
\label{prop:hull}
Let $\Zn^{h}$ be the hull graph and $W^{h}$ its greatest fixpoint. Then
$\Omega \subseteq W^{h}$, so $W^{h}_v = \emptyset$ for all $v \models p \wedge \lnot q$ implies
\texttt{p --> q} \textsc{holds}. The converse fails, so a non-empty $W^h$ concludes nothing and the
algorithm must \textsc{abstain}. \proved{}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:sound-holds} it suffices that $F^h$ over-approximate $F$ termwise.
$\Reach(v) \subseteq \Zn_v \subseteq \Zn^h_v$ since the hull is extensive. The edge set of the hull
vertex is a superset of the edge set of each merged zone, because an edge is kept when its guard
meets the zone and the hull contains each of them, so every $\Pre_e$ disjunct survives and more may
appear. For $\mathrm{stop}$: $\mathrm{time\text{-}diverges}$ is monotone in the zone (delay and
invariant intersection are), and \texttt{state\_has\_deadlock} is monotone too, since enabledness is
a per-valuation property, so a valuation stuck in a merged zone is still stuck in the hull. The
converse fails because a certificate may rest entirely on hull points outside $\Reach(v)$, and the
simulation argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:asym} does not apply: an arbitrary hull point need not be
$\simlu$-simulated by a reachable one.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The one non-monotone term, and why it is harmless]
\label{rem:hull-adj}
$\mathrm{adj}(v) = \emptyset$ is \emph{not} monotone --- a hull can acquire successors that no
merged zone had --- so it could shrink $\mathrm{stop}$ and break Proposition~\ref{prop:hull}. It is
subsumed by \texttt{state\_has\_deadlock}: an empty adjacency means every valuation of the zone is
stuck, which that per-valuation test reports. The partial-deadlock term must therefore \textbf{not}
be disabled in this mode. \proved{}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Algorithm 4: racing the two lanes}
\label{sec:otf:d7}

The two directions of the verdict are proved by approximations that are sound in \emph{opposite}
directions, and each lane may therefore make the approximation that is free for its own direction:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
lane & approximates & may conclude \\
\midrule
D5 (Algorithm 2) & \textbf{under} --- confined post-fixpoint, $aLU$ graph & \textsc{violated}, on a fragment \\
D6 (Algorithm 3) & \textbf{over} --- one hull zone per discrete state & \textsc{holds}, at the discrete floor \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Run both and take whichever concludes first. This is sound because they decide the same proposition
by approximations that cannot both be wrong in the same direction, and it is the same argument the
engine's existing $aLU$-versus-exact race already rests on. It is strictly stronger than either
lane: \S\ref{sec:otf:eval} exhibits a query that \emph{neither} Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} nor D5 can
answer at all and that D6 settles.
