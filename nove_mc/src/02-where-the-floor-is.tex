\section{Where the floor is, and where it is not}
\label{sec:floor}

This section is measurement, not argument. Its purpose is to establish that the two algorithms in
this document attack the only two surfaces that are actually above their floor, and to state
plainly the (large) set of places where they are worth nothing.

Setup: engine \texttt{./build-mostopt/ptctlmc} with \texttt{PTCTLMC\_STATS=1}; reference
\verifyta{} 5.0.0 with \texttt{-u -S0}; 12 logical cores. Where a run did not terminate it is
marked \textsc{incomplete} and is not counted as anything.

\subsection{\texttt{A[]} reachability: on the floor, and hard against every zone-level lever}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
model & $|\Disc|$ & stored & explored & generated & gen/stored \\
\midrule
fischer8   & 25\,080  & 25\,080  & 40\,536  & 177\,000     & 7.06 \\
fischer10  & 260\,998 & 260\,998 & 447\,598 & 2\,372\,190  & 9.09 \\
twolock10  & 271\,238 & 271\,238 & 457\,838 & 2\,428\,510  & 8.95 \\
arbiter15  & 278\,528 & 278\,528 & 278\,528 & 2\,457\,600  & \textbf{8.82} \\
viking12   & 241\,662 & 241\,662 & 241\,662 & 688\,128     & 2.85 \\
csma10     & 22\,784  & 22\,784  & 22\,784  & 147\,200     & 6.46 \\
phil12     & 531\,440 & 574\,234 & 798\,574 & 6\,086\,960  & 10.60 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\measured{}

\noindent
\texttt{stored} equals $|\Disc|$ on every family but \texttt{phil}, and equals \verifyta{}'s count
exactly. Prior work in this project establishes, six independent ways, that this is a hard floor:
thread count leaves \texttt{fischer8} bit-identical from 1 to 12 threads; \verifyta{}'s search-order
flags give identical counts and DFS is $31.6\%$ \emph{worse} on \texttt{phil}; a perfect non-convex
federation merge removes zero zones on five families; and \verifyta{}'s maximal over-approximation
\texttt{-A} changes \texttt{fischer10} by zero on both axes. We do not re-litigate any of that. We
take it as given.

Two surfaces are nevertheless \emph{not} on their floor.

\paragraph{(i) \texttt{explored}, on \texttt{fischer} and \texttt{twolock} only.}
$\texttt{explored} - \texttt{stored}$ equals, to the state, the number of \emph{cross-level
retirements}: zones that were stored, expanded, and then superseded by a more general zone for the
same discrete state arriving some levels later. That is $41.7\%$ of all expansions on
\texttt{fischer10} and $40.8\%$ on \texttt{twolock10}, and exactly $0\%$ on \texttt{arbiter},
\texttt{viking} and \texttt{csma}. \S\ref{sec:deferral} attacks this.

\paragraph{(ii) \texttt{generated}.}
The last column above is worth a sentence because it is easy to miss. Even on the three families
that are at the absolute floor on both reported axes, the engine \emph{materialises} between
$2.85$ and $8.82$ successor zones for every one it stores --- \texttt{arbiter15} computes
$2\,457\,600$ DBMs to keep $278\,528$. This is a real surface, but it is a \emph{work} surface, not
a state surface, and this document does not claim it as a state-count result.

\subsection{Liveness: not on the floor, and losing to \uppaal{} badly}
\label{sec:floor:liveness}

All eleven benchmark queries are \texttt{A[]}. Liveness had never been benchmarked, and it is a
different story.

Measurement note, because the naive number is wrong: the engine's liveness path does not run one
search. It runs an $aLU$ search and then, if that finds no witness, \emph{races} two lanes that
decide the same proposition. Both lanes share one statistics object, so the CLI figure is a sum
over whichever lanes ran. All figures below use \texttt{PTCTLMC\_NORACE=1 PTCTLMC\_LTDEBUG=1} and
read the unambiguous per-search line.

Query \texttt{C1.cs --> C1.idle}, which is \emph{satisfied} and therefore forces exhaustive search:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
\toprule
model & floor $|\Disc|$ & liveness nodes & zones/disc & \uppaal{} stored & \uppaal{}/floor & engine/\uppaal{} \\
\midrule
arbiter4 & 48  & 4\,096  & \textbf{85.3}  & 297   & 6.2$\times$  & 13.8$\times$ worse \\
arbiter5 & 112 & 68\,102 & \textbf{608.1} & 1\,671& 14.9$\times$ & 40.8$\times$ worse \\
arbiter6 & 256 & \multicolumn{5}{l}{\textsc{incomplete} --- killed after 15 minutes} \\
csma4    & 104 & 3\,341  & \textbf{32.1}  & 401   & 3.9$\times$  & 8.3$\times$ worse \\
csma5    & 272 & 60\,271 & \textbf{221.6} & 2\,491& 9.2$\times$  & 24.2$\times$ worse \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\measured{}

Three readings:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The engine stores $13.8\times$ and $40.8\times$ more per discrete state than \uppaal{}, and
        the gap \emph{widens} with instance size. This is the exact mirror image of the
        reachability result.
  \item \uppaal{} itself is $3.9$--$14.9\times$ above the reachability floor, and also widening. So
        the available headroom is not ``catch up with \uppaal{}''; it is an order of magnitude that
        neither tool takes.
  \item The cause is structural. Node identity for liveness must be exact zone equality, because
        subsumption creates the spurious cycles of \S\ref{sec:setting:subedge}. The engine does
        implement a restricted, liveness-compatible subsumption, and its own source records why it
        is not the default: it is sound but \emph{not complete}, since an absorbed newcomer is
        never expanded and a witness that exists only below it is never looked for.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Target, numerically.} A sound and complete liveness procedure storing $\bigO(1)$ zones per
reachable discrete state would beat \uppaal{} by $6.2\times$ on \texttt{arbiter4} and $14.9\times$
on \texttt{arbiter5} on \texttt{States stored}, with the factor growing --- while the forward search
that produces its graph is already at the proved floor.
\end{quote}

\S\ref{sec:liveness} gives such a procedure and \S\ref{sec:eval} measures it hitting that target
exactly.

\subsection{What this document does not claim}

For honesty, and because the negative space is most of the space:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Nothing here reduces \texttt{stored} on \texttt{A[]}. That floor stands.
  \item The liveness algorithm is worth nothing on \emph{violated} queries, where the existing
        engine already answers within a handful of nodes.
  \item The deferral policy is worth nothing on \texttt{arbiter}, \texttt{viking} and \texttt{csma},
        which have no retirements at all.
  \item Neither algorithm's payoff is proportional to how replicated the model is; neither is a
        symmetry reduction, a partial-order reduction, or a coarser abstraction.
\end{itemize}
