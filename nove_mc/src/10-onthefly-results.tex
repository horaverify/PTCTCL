\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:otf:eval}

Two query sets: 48 queries over \texttt{arbiter4/5}, \texttt{fischer4/5}, \texttt{csma4/5},
\texttt{phil4}, \texttt{twolock4}, \texttt{viking4}, \texttt{grc4}; and 32 over the larger members
of the same families. The metric is \textbf{symbolic states stored when the verdict is produced}.
Ratios are geometric means of the per-query ratio.

\subsection{Against Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp}}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
 & \multicolumn{2}{c}{violated} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{satisfied} \\
\cmidrule(lr){2-3}\cmidrule(lr){4-5}
 & 39 small & 23 large & 9 small & 6 large \\
\midrule
Algorithm~2 vs Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} & $12.6\times$ & $\mathbf{145\times}$ & $1.0\times$ & $1.0\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The saving is confined to violated queries, and it grows with the model --- $145\times$ on the
larger set, best case $4022\times$ (\texttt{fischer8 P0.idle --> P0.cs}, $36202$ states against
$9$). On satisfied queries there is nothing to save: \texttt{p --> q} holds only if \emph{every}
reachable $p \wedge \lnot q$ state fails to have an infinite $\lnot q$ run, so the reachable set
must be enumerated, and the final pass is Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} exactly. \measured{}

\subsection{Against \uppaal{}}
\label{sec:otf:uppaal}

\verifyta{} 5.0.0 at its defaults, since defaults are what a user gets.

\begin{center}
\textbf{48 of 48 verdicts agree. Zero disagreements.} \measured{}
\end{center}

This is the strongest validation here: an independent implementation of a different algorithm.

\subsection{The accounting, and why it differs by verdict class}
\label{sec:otf:counters}

There is an obvious objection to any explored-state gap on a violated query: both tools stop at the
first witness, so neither can ``finish early''. The resolution is that \verifyta{}'s leads-to
counters \textbf{do not include the nested inevitability check at all}. Compare \texttt{E<> p} ---
pure reachability to the $p$-state, no liveness whatsoever --- against \texttt{p --> q}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrr@{}}
\toprule
model & query & stored & explored \\
\midrule
\texttt{fischer4} & \texttt{E<> P0.req}          & 5 & 1 \\
\texttt{fischer4} & \texttt{P0.req --> P0.cs}    & 5 & 1 \\
\texttt{csma4}    & \texttt{E<> S0.Send}         & 5 & 1 \\
\texttt{csma4}    & \texttt{S0.Send --> S0.Wait} & 5 & 1 \\
\texttt{phil4}    & \texttt{E<> P0.Eat}          & 9 & 2 \\
\texttt{phil4}    & \texttt{P0.Eat --> P0.Think} & 9 & 2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A liveness query costing \emph{exactly} what plain reachability to its $p$-state costs is possible
only if the $EG$ check is uncounted. \texttt{grc4} differs ($5/1$ against $860/463$) only because
its first several $p$-states do not violate, so the \emph{outer} search runs longer; the nested
checks are still uncounted. It also explains the counter inversion: \verifyta{} reports
stored $>$ explored on leads-to ($860 > 463$) but stored $<$ explored on \texttt{A[] true}
($1619 < 3273$). \measured{}

The comparison therefore applies \textbf{the same rule to both sides}, and the right rule is not
the same for the two verdict classes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Violated: outer-only.} The search stops at the witness, so the inner $EG$ work is
        genuinely extra --- work \emph{neither} tool's counter reports. Our split attributes to
        the inner check everything that exists only to decide $EG\lnot q$ at an accepting vertex,
        including that vertex's own expansion, which the outer reachability never needs because
        the search stops there. The initial state is not counted, matching \verifyta{}: its
        \texttt{E<> p} figures are exactly our count minus one on every query measured.
  \item \textbf{Satisfied: total.} Both tools must build the whole reachable graph, so nodes first
        reached by the inner DFS are work that is needed regardless. Reporting outer-only here
        would give $973$ instead of $1790$ and would \emph{understate our real cost}. It is not
        used.
\end{itemize}

Applying outer-only to the violated class turns all six of the earlier ``losses'' into exact ties
--- they were the queries whose entire difference was the inner check we counted and \verifyta{}
did not --- and leaves \texttt{grc4} a $5.6\times$ win.

\subsection{Why the margin is asymmetric}

$5$--$40\times$ on satisfied queries against ties on violated ones is not a weakness on the
violated side; it is that the two classes have different amounts to win.

A satisfied query must enumerate the reachable set, so an advantage in \emph{representation} ---
$aLU$ subsumption plus the exact merge, against \verifyta{}'s zone store --- compounds over every
state, and the factor grows with the model. A violated query stops after a handful of states:
on 31 of 39 both tools settle at the same state, and there is essentially nothing to be better at.
The exception proves it --- \texttt{grc4 T0.near --> T0.in} is the one violated query needing real
search ($118$ expansions), and it is exactly where the representation advantage reappears, at
$5.6\times$.

\subsection{Limits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Federation width.} The backward operator's real failure mode is a federation that
        splits without bound. \S\ref{sec:otf:closure} removed the two implementation causes, and no
        query in either set now caps --- but the bound is a resource limit, not a theorem, and a
        capped pass yields no verdict (Remark~\ref{rem:capped}).
  \item \textbf{Order sensitivity.} \texttt{grc4 T0.near --> T0.in} moves by a factor of three
        under different frontier orders, and so does \verifyta{} ($463$ at \texttt{-o 0}, $6559$ at
        \texttt{-o 1}). Both tools are guessing.
  \item \textbf{Diagonal constraints.} $aLU$ is unusable with diagonal guards, and the algorithm
        falls back to exact inclusion, losing the floor.
  \item \textbf{Inherited incompleteness.} The whole-zone stopping convention is forced by
        LU-extrapolation (a per-valuation stuck test is unsound on extrapolated zones), and is the
        same on both sides of every comparison here.
\end{itemize}
