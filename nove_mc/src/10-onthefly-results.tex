\section{Evaluation of the on-the-fly algorithms}
\label{sec:otf:eval}

Baseline throughout is Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp}, the three-phase version, on the same models with the
same $aLU$ subsumption. The metric is \textbf{symbolic states stored at the moment the verdict is
produced}. Two query sets: 48 queries over
\texttt{arbiter4/5}, \texttt{fischer4/5}, \texttt{csma4/5}, \texttt{phil4}, \texttt{twolock4},
\texttt{viking4}, \texttt{grc4}; and 32 queries over the larger members of the same families.
Ratios are geometric means of the per-query ratio.

Three of the five directions lose, and it is worth being explicit about why. \measured{}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{D4 is exactly $1.00\times$ by construction}: it does not change the search, only
        when three predicate evaluations per vertex happen, against a successor computation that
        costs a delay, a constrain, a reset set, a canonicalisation and an extrapolation.
  \item \textbf{D1 and D2 lose $11$--$16\%$ on average and up to $2.5\times$} (\texttt{grc4}
        $2345 \to 5215$, \texttt{fischer5} $762 \to 1126$). Append-only buys immutable vertices and
        pays the antichain for them, and by Remark~\ref{rem:d1-needs-d2} D1 cannot avoid that price.
  \item \textbf{D5 dominates D3} wherever the witness is deep, because D3's trigger is ``after every
        BFS level'' while D5's is ``a candidate actually appeared'':
        \texttt{fischer8 P0.req} $165 \to 46$, \texttt{csma8 S0.Send} $277 \to 82$,
        \texttt{fischer5 P0.req} $56 \to 22$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Against the shipping forward engine}
\label{sec:otf:vsfwd}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} is the right baseline for the fixpoint, but the tool a user actually has is
the lazy forward Tarjan, which is \emph{already} on-the-fly. The like-for-like metric is D5's
expansions against that engine's \texttt{explored}. Verdict agreement is $47/48$; the $48^{\text{th}}$
is a \emph{timeout of the forward engine}, not a disagreement. \measured{}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
 & geo-mean & extremes \\
\midrule
violated (39)  & $1.18\times$ fewer & $1.00\times$ -- $5.4\times$ \\
satisfied (8)  & $179\times$ fewer  & $8.1\times$ -- $1216\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

D5 is \emph{never} worse on a violated query, and $31$ of $39$ are dead on $1.00\times$: two correct
on-the-fly searches stopping at the same state, which is what should happen when the witness is
shallow. The gaps are exactly the queries whose witness is a \textbf{cycle} --- \texttt{grc4
T0.near --> T0.in} $9379 \to 1722$, \texttt{fischer5 P0.cs --> P0.idle} $116 \to 56$,
\texttt{twolock4 P0.waitB --> P0.cs} $125 \to 64$ --- which is \S\ref{sec:otf:d5} being paid out:
the forward engine must refuse subsumption edges to argue that a run is infinite, and walks further
for it.

\begin{remark}[A truncation in the forward engine, found by this comparison]
\label{rem:truncation}
The forward engine's outer BFS skipped a frontier vertex that was already expanded. That
\texttt{continue} also skipped \emph{publishing the vertex's successors}, and the inner Tarjan
expands whole regions before the outer search reaches them, so those regions never entered the
frontier. Against the discrete counts of \texttt{benchmarks/parity.csv}, which are verified against
\verifyta{}: \texttt{csma4} reached $21$ of $104$ reachable discrete states and answered
\textsc{holds} from that fifth of the graph; \texttt{fischer4} reached $217$ of $220$;
\texttt{arbiter4} was unaffected. This is \textbf{soundness-relevant}, not a performance artefact: a
$p \wedge \lnot q$ state may be reachable \emph{only through} $q$ states, which the inner search
never enters, so the outer BFS is the only thing that can find it. No wrong verdict is exhibited on
the current benchmark set. \measured{}

An earlier revision of this section, written before the repair, reported \texttt{csma4}/\texttt{csma5}
as satisfied-query \emph{losses} for D5 ($128$ vs $42$, $352$ vs $62$). Those baselines were the
truncated search. Repaired they are $6682$ and $120542$, so D5 wins by $52\times$ and $342\times$,
and no satisfied query in the set is a loss. The earlier claim is \textbf{retracted}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The hull lane}
\label{sec:otf:eval:hull}

Algorithm~3 was scored as the one-sided procedure it is: the only failure that matters is claiming
\textsc{holds} when the query is violated.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
outcome over 48 queries & count \\
\midrule
unsound (claimed a verdict it may not) & \textbf{0} \\
proved \textsc{holds} (the useful outcome) & 9 of 9 \\
abstained on a violated query (correct) & 39 of 39 \\
abstained on a satisfied query (sound, useless) & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

It never abstained on a query that holds --- the coarsest zone abstraction available is
\emph{still} precise enough to prove every satisfied liveness property in this set --- and it stores
exactly one symbolic state per reachable discrete state:

\begin{center}

\bottomrule

The factor is $1.0$ wherever the $aLU$ graph was already at one zone per discrete state --- six of
the seven families --- and $16\times$ on \texttt{grc4}, the one family where it was not
($18.32$ zones per discrete state). \measured{}

\subsection{The query nothing else can answer}
\label{sec:eval:cap}

\texttt{csma8 Bus.Active --> Bus.Idle} exhausts the federation-width cap in every exact lane. It is
reported \textsc{unusable} under Remark~\ref{rem:capped}, and raising the cap does not rescue it: at
$2\times 10^{6}$ it had not converged after ten minutes.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrl@{}}
\toprule
algorithm & verdict & states & zones/disc.\ state \\
\midrule
Alg.~\ref{alg:gfp} & \textsc{unusable} (cap) & 5888 & 1.44 \\
D5                 & \textsc{unusable} (cap) & 5888 & 1.44 \\
D6 (hull)          & \textbf{\textsc{holds}} & 4096 & \textbf{1.00} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The hull does not merely store fewer states, it \textbf{removes the failure mode}: each vertex's
value stays a single convex zone, so the federation has nothing to split. Federation explosion, not
state count, is the exact backward operator's real limit, and the coarse lane is immune to it by
construction. \measured{}

Two further \texttt{csma8} queries are \textsc{unusable} for Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} and settled
exactly by D3 and D5, in $1$ and $12$ expansions respectively --- matching the forward engine's own
expansion counts --- because the certificate lives on a fragment where the fixpoint is trivial while
the full-graph fixpoint explodes. \measured{}

\subsection{Where the hull lane fails}
\label{sec:otf:eval:hullfail}

Algorithm~3 is not a replacement, and the failure is not marginal. On \texttt{fischer} it is both
imprecise and expensive:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
query & Alg.~\ref{alg:gfp} & D5 & D6 \\
\midrule
\texttt{fischer6 P0.req --> P0.wait} (holds)     & 2694 / 0.04\,s & 2939 / 0.03\,s & 2378 / 0.71\,s \\
\texttt{fischer7 P0.req --> P0.wait} (holds)     & 9798 / 0.20\,s & 11598 / 0.50\,s & 7737 / \textbf{35.5\,s} \\
\texttt{fischer7 P0.idle --> P0.cs} (violated)   & 9798 / 0.31\,s & 8 / 0.00\,s & abstain, 7737 / 16.7\,s \\
\texttt{fischer8 P0.idle --> P0.cs} (violated)   & 36202 / 1.65\,s & 9 / 0.00\,s & \textbf{timeout} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two distinct problems. \emph{Precision}: \texttt{fischer}'s satisfied proof rests on a correlation
between clocks that the hull discards, so the fixpoint has to work much harder to reach $\emptyset$.
\emph{Cost}: on a violated query the lane pays the whole hull graph before abstaining, which is
pure loss. This is why Algorithm~4 races the lanes rather than sequencing them, and it identifies
the open problem: an abstraction strictly between $aLU$ and the hull --- coarse enough to collapse
\texttt{grc4}'s $18.32$ zones per discrete state, precise enough to keep \texttt{fischer}'s
correlations. \openq{}

\subsection{Differential validation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item 48 queries $\times$ 7 modes and 32 queries $\times$ 3 modes: \textbf{0 disagreements} among
        runs that converged. \measured{}
  \item Mode \texttt{base} reproduces Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp}'s stored count on \emph{every} query
        in both sets, which is what licenses reading the other columns as differences of algorithm
        rather than of reimplementation. \measured{}
  \item Both \texttt{csma8} certificates were checked against the shipping engine independently.
\end{itemize}

Two defects in the on-the-fly code were found by this harness and are recorded because both are
easy to reintroduce. First, D5's outer BFS skipped vertices the inner DFS had already expanded and
therefore failed to publish their successors --- the same defect as
Remark~\ref{rem:truncation}, independently committed --- which produced plausible \textsc{holds}
verdicts on fewer states. Second, a capped pass was read as ``no certificate'', converting an
exhausted budget into a silent \textsc{holds}; see Remark~\ref{rem:capped}.

\subsection{Two orthogonal improvements}

\textbf{Accepting-closure restriction.} $F$ is defined per vertex from its own successors, so the
value at an accepting vertex depends only on the $\lnot q$-forward-closure of the accepting set.
Restricting the pass to that closure is \emph{exact}, not an approximation, and applies to
Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} unchanged: \texttt{grc4 T0.in --> T0.far} $5.87\,\text{s} \to
2.03\,\text{s}$, \texttt{grc4 G.up --> G.low} $0.13\,\text{s} \to 0.02\,\text{s}$. \measured{}

\textbf{Refutation budget.} On every violated query measured, D5 confirms its certificate on the
\emph{first} candidate (one pass, one to three $\Pre$ calls, including \texttt{grc4 T0.near -->
T0.in} where the DFS first walks 1722 states). Only queries that hold refute repeatedly. Spending a
fixed budget and then abandoning early exit cannot change a verdict --- it defers the answer to the
exact final pass --- and cut \texttt{grc4 T0.in --> T0.far} from $9.31\,\text{s}$ / 22 passes to
$3.43\,\text{s}$ / 9 passes. \measured{}
