\section{Related work, and an honest narrowing of the claim}
\label{sec:related}

An adversarial prior-art search was run against this document's own claims. It killed most of the
first draft's framing. This section records what it killed, because the surviving claim is much
narrower and much more defensible than the one we started with.

\subsection{What is not new, and must not be claimed}

\paragraph{Greatest fixpoints for liveness.}
$\mathsf{EG}$ as a greatest fixpoint, and fair-$\mathsf{EG}$ as a nested fixpoint, are Emerson and
Lei (LICS 1986). The Emerson--Lei algorithm is the canonical \emph{SCC-hull} method for fair-cycle
detection, and Somenzi, Ravi and Bloem (FMCAD 2002) prove it complexity-optimal among algorithms
derivable from the Generalized SCC Hull. Ravi, Bloem and Somenzi (FMCAD 2000) is the comparative
study. Nothing about ``use a greatest fixpoint'' is new.

\paragraph{The leads-to encoding.}
$\texttt{p --> q}$ is violated iff $\mathsf{EF}(p \wedge \mathsf{EG}\lnot q)$, by elementary CTL
duality. This is how \uppaal{} defines the operator. Not new.

\paragraph{Restricting the fixpoint to the reachable set.}
This is the one that hurt most. Computing forward reachability first and then using it to restrict
or simplify the CTL fixpoints --- including the greatest ones --- is not merely known, it is
shipped behaviour: it is the \textbf{default} in VIS (\texttt{model\_check -d 1}, unreachable states
as don't-cares), a first-class flag in NuSMV (\texttt{-f} / \texttt{compute\_reachable}), and the
subject of Moon, Jang, Hachtel, Somenzi, Yuan and Pixley, \emph{Approximate Reachability Don't Cares
for CTL Model Checking} (ICCAD 1998). Not new.

\paragraph{Chaotic iteration as the route to parallelism.}
Also not new, and with a precise attribution. Cousot's 1977 report on asynchronous iterative methods
proves convergence for monotone equation systems over a complete lattice with no synchronisation
between processes. Cohen, Namjoshi, Sa'ar, Zuck and Kisyova (HVC 2010) apply exactly this argument
to parallelise a symbolic model-checking fixpoint, citing Cousot, and state that any fair schedule
suffices so threads may run at different rates with very loose synchronisation. The complementary
half --- that DFS is inherently sequential (Reif 1985), so linear-time LTL algorithms resist
parallelisation and one should prefer BFS/fixpoint-shaped algorithms --- is the organising argument
of the survey chapter by Barnat, Bloemen, Duret-Lutz, Laarman, Petrucci, van de Pol and Renault
(\emph{Handbook of Parallel Constraint Reasoning}, 2018). \S\ref{sec:liveness:parallel} of this
document should therefore be read as \emph{applying} that argument, not as making it.

\paragraph{Reachability-then-shrinking-fixpoint, in parallel.}
That is OWCTY (Fisler, Fraer, Kamhi, Vardi, Yang, TACAS 2001), whose presentation in the survey
above initialises the state set with a forward reachability pass and then iterates elimination
rounds restricted to it. Structurally the same shape as Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp}. Not new.

\subsection{What the timed literature does, and why it does not do this}

The timed-automata side is a separate lineage and it is DFS-shaped throughout. Tripakis studied
emptiness for timed B\"uchi automata; Herbreteau, Srivathsan and Walukiewicz developed better and
lazy abstractions; Herbreteau, Srivathsan, Tran and Walukiewicz (FSTTCS 2016) is titled, in
substance, \emph{why liveness for timed automata is hard}; and Laarman, Olesen, Dalsgaard, Larsen
and van de Pol (CAV 2013) give multi-core emptiness checking of timed B\"uchi automata using
inclusion abstraction. The engine measured here implements the last of these.

The shared premise of that whole line is the one stated in \S\ref{sec:setting:subedge}:
\textbf{zone subsumption is unsound for B\"uchi emptiness}, because redirected edges close cycles
the concrete system cannot execute. The published responses are to \emph{weaken} subsumption until
it is safe --- which the engine's own source records as sound but not complete, since an absorbed
newcomer is never expanded --- or to \emph{abandon} it and store exact zones, which is the
$32$--$608\times$ blow-up measured in \S\ref{sec:floor:liveness}.

\subsection{Parallel backward analysis of timed automata is also not new: ZEUS}
\label{sec:related:zeus}

This one nearly took the paper with it, and it was surfaced by a note in the project's own records
rather than by the literature search.

\textbf{ZEUS} (Braberman, Olivero, Schapachnik) is a \emph{distributed} timed model checker built on
Kronos which performs \textbf{backwards} computation over timed automata --- presented at the
Workshop on Parallel and Distributed Model Checking (CONCUR 2002), with a later journal treatment of
its practical limitations (\emph{Formal Methods in System Design}, 2006) and companion work on
workload prediction and redistribution. It partitions the state space by assigning each automaton
location to one processor. The Kronos lineage is explicit that the backward algorithm, not the
forward one, is what supports arbitrary TCTL formulae and controller synthesis.

So the sentence ``nobody has parallelised backward timed-automata search'' is \textbf{false}, and
this document does not say it. Parallel backward zone analysis for timed automata is roughly
twenty-five years old.

What distinguishes Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp} is not that it is backward, nor that it is parallel, but
\emph{what graph it runs on}. Kronos-lineage backward analysis computes over the state space
directly; it has no forward phase with an $LU$/$aLU$ simulation abstraction, because that machinery
postdates it and belongs to the forward, \uppaal{}-lineage engines. The claim here is about the
\emph{interaction} between that abstraction and the direction of the fixpoint --- which cannot arise
in a tool that has no such abstraction.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{NOT ESTABLISHED IN SOURCES.} Whether ZEUS or its successors combine a forward reachability
pass with the backward computation, and in particular whether any of them confine the backward
fixpoint to a \emph{subsumption-closed} forward-reachable graph, could not be settled: the primary
texts were behind paywalls or returned HTTP 403. Secondary summaries mention combining forward and
backward analysis, but no retrieved source states that the confining set is subsumption-closed. This
is the single most important open question about the novelty of \S\ref{sec:liveness} and it should be
settled from the primary sources before publication.
\end{quote}

\subsection{The claim that survives}

\begin{quote}
The obstacle is an artefact of searching \textbf{forward}. In a backward greatest fixpoint the
redirected edge is harmless, because the target's value enters only as an \emph{upper bound}, and
over-approximating an upper bound preserves the containment soundness needs
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sound-holds}). Consequently liveness can be decided on the \textbf{fully
$aLU$-subsumed} zone graph --- at the reachability floor, $1.00$ zones per reachable discrete state
--- rather than on an exact-zone or weakened-subsumption graph.
\end{quote}

This is what we claim, and only this. It is specific to timed automata: the untimed symbolic
literature above has no notion of a subsumption edge at all, because BDD-based reachability does no
zone subsumption, so the question this document answers cannot arise there. And the timed
literature, which does have the notion, has approached liveness through DFS-shaped emptiness
checking, where the answer is genuinely negative.

The prior-art search found \textbf{no work combining} (a) zone-based timed liveness, (b) a
chaotically iterated fixpoint, and (c) restriction to the already-\emph{subsumed}
forward-reachable zone graph. Given \S\ref{sec:related:zeus}, the emphasis belongs entirely on
(c) and on the asymmetry theorem that licenses it: backwardness and parallelism are both old, and
the contribution is the observation that the abstraction which makes forward liveness unsound is
exactly the one a backward fixpoint can keep.

\subsection{Adjacent results worth positioning against}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Emerson--Lei optimality} (Somenzi, Ravi, Bloem, FMCAD 2002). Algorithm~\ref{alg:gfp}
        does not claim a better fixpoint schedule than EL; it claims a smaller \emph{graph} to run a
        fixpoint over. Those are orthogonal, and the improvement here is entirely in the graph.
  \item \textbf{OWCTY} achieves a similar shape by topological-sort elimination rather than
        asynchronous relaxation, and on an unabstracted state space.
  \item \textbf{Deterministic parallel fixpoint} (Kim, Venet, Thakur, POPL 2020) pushes the opposite
        way --- towards deterministic schedules, because chaotic ones give schedule-dependent
        \emph{intermediate} results. The fixpoint itself is schedule-independent, which is all
        \S\ref{sec:liveness:parallel} uses.
  \item A reviewer will ask, correctly, whether the benchmark suite exercises this at all, since all
        eleven shipped queries are \texttt{A[]}. \S\ref{sec:floor:liveness} answers that: they do
        not, which is why liveness was unmeasured, and the 164 differential queries of
        \S\ref{sec:eval} were written for this document.
\end{itemize}
